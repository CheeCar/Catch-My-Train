# Features

## Modes

There are four modes. Each has its own view, and modes are never mixed together.

- **Subway:** subways, light rail, trolleys and trams (such as the Roosevelt Island Tram)
- **Bus:** local and express buses
- **Train:** commuter and intercity trains
- **Ferry:** passenger and car ferries

Each metro area's page in [Data sources](data-sources.md) lists every operator there, what it runs and whether it has real-time data.

Under the mode's name, the top of the sidebar says which kinds of vehicles the mode has in the metro area you walk from (for example "Subway · Light Rail · Tram" in the New York Metropolitan Area). Click it to see that area's operators, each marked **Live** or **Timetable**, and the ones without times (see [Stations without times](#stations-without-times)). **Data Source…** there opens the full list of operators in Settings.

## Stations without times

Some operators, such as AirTrain JFK and several ferries, don't publish their times in a form Catch My Train can use; each metro area's page in [Data sources](data-sources.md) lists them. Their stations and piers are still listed, so you know they are there:

- In the station list, each is under its mode (AirTrain JFK under Subway, the piers under Ferry). They come after the stations with departures in the same range, with the operator's name and a gray **No times** tag.
- Walk times to them are straight-line estimates, marked "estimated". Where a short crossing has a pier on each side of the water (North Ferry, for example), the station list shows only the pier on your side; the map shows both.
- The station page, the map's card and Plan a Trip say why there are no times and, where the operator's website says so, how often it runs. **Open Website** opens the operator's page for that station. On the map they are gray dots.
- The menu bar and the widgets show only stations with departures, so they leave these out.

Private commuter buses aren't included at all, because their times aren't published in a form Catch My Train can use. The list of Bus operators at the top of the sidebar says so.

## First launch

The **Ready to Go** window explains three optional things and what works without them: location, Open at login, and the desktop widget (with step-by-step help to add it). Until you finish it, the app downloads nothing and doesn't ask for your location. Until you finish it, the main window offers **Show Setup Again** and **Start**, which starts the app right away. The window can be opened again from Settings → Privacy → Show Setup Again.

## Main window

- **Nearby stations** on the left, each on its own card with its lines and the walk time. The selected station's card is blue.
- **Commute presets**, if you made any, are a row of buttons under the starting place: one click switches the starting place and the mode. The one in use is blue. A preset that can't be used now is grayed out, and its tip says why (its favorite was removed, or location is off).
- Under each station is the **first departure you can catch**: its line, the minutes until it comes and the verdict's icon. In Subway mode there is one for each direction (↑ and ↓), and a direction with departures but none you can catch shows "—"; in the other modes it is the earliest one of both directions, and the line is left out when there is none. The minutes always match the station's page on the right.
- Above the stations, a line shows your primary range, extended range and arrivals window, each after its icon (for example "15 min · 20 min · 2 hr"), on more lines when the sidebar is narrow. Click it to see the details. The plus button at the end of the line is **Extend Range for Now**; while the range is extended, the line says by how much (for example "+10 min for now").
- **Departures in both directions** on the right, side by side and lined up row by row, labeled "To …" (Uptown and Downtown for the NYC subway). A direction with more than two terminals names the first two and "and N more"; click its title on the station page to see them all. A bus loop that ends where it starts is labeled with its farthest stop when it leaves its first stop. They can be narrowed to one direction; at a station where that direction has no departures and the other has some, the other is shown under it.
- **Filter by line:** click a line's badge to show only that line. You can choose several; the lines you didn't choose turn gray. When a station has more lines than fit in one row, a menu at the end of the row lists them all, with a check mark beside the ones you chose, and its button shows how many that is.
- For every departure: the train's **time** in large type with the countdown right after it, then your walk time and the verdict (*Can try*, *Tight*, *Too soon*, *Not sure*). In each direction, the first departure you can catch has a bar beside it, as in the menu bar panel and the widget. When a departure is *Not sure*, it also names the next one you can catch.
- Click a departure, or select it and press Space, to see the details: the walk, the time from the station's entrance to the platform ("Time to board" for ferries and buses, "Ticket and security check" for the Statue of Liberty Ferry), the minutes to spare and when the prediction was updated. For a departure you can try for, they also say when to leave at the latest, with **Remind Me at …** (see [Leave-by reminders](#leave-by-reminders)) and **Directions**.
- **Directions** beside the walk at the top of a station's page opens the walking route there in Apple Maps, or in Google Maps if you choose it in Settings → Privacy. It isn't there when network use is off or there is no starting place yet.
- **Service tags:** Local, Express, Limited and SBS.
- A time that comes from a timetable, not from real-time data, is tagged **Timetable**. When every time in a direction is a timetable time, the tag appears once, beside the direction's title.
- **Arrivals window:** only departures in the next 30 minutes to 4 hours are shown (2 hours by default; **Show arrivals within** in Settings). When a direction has none in that window, it also shows the next one when the app already knows it (for example "Next: 6:05 AM"); nothing is downloaded for this.
- The countdown updates every 15 seconds.
- At the bottom: the metro area's name, and which of the selected station's operators have live times now. **Updated …** shows the time of the data. When the last refresh brought nothing newer, two lines show both times instead: **Data updated** and **Last refresh**. **Timetables up to date as of …** shows how current the selected station's timetables are: the oldest of its operators' last successful downloads. The last line names the operators whose data the station shows. With network use turned off, **Updated …** gives only the time of live data downloaded before, or nothing.
- **When there is nothing to show,** the page says why and offers the next step as a button: **Extend Range for Now**, **Choose a Place…** and **Settings…** (it opens Search range) when no station is nearby, **Choose a Place…** outside the service area or when there is no starting place yet ("No place chosen yet", "Location access is off" or "Couldn't find your location"), **Settings…** when network use is turned off (it opens Privacy), **Refresh** when a download failed or something else went wrong, and **Start** on standby, when this mode's timetables haven't been downloaded yet (they are downloaded the first time you turn the app on). The sidebar says why in one line.
- The **Plan a Trip** and map buttons at the top of the sidebar open their own windows. They sit side by side when there is room.
- While leave-by reminders are set, a bell with their number is at the front of the toolbar.

## Places

- Walk from **my current location** or from **a place I choose**.
- Search addresses and places, pick a point on the map, or use one of 10 recent places or up to 50 favorites (favorites can also be added on the transit map). Drag favorites to change their order, or use **Move Left** and **Move Right** in a favorite's right-click menu (⌥⌘← and ⌥⌘→ while it's selected); Plan a Trip's star button lists the first 6 in this order.
- The search also finds the current mode's stations by name, from the app's own data, so they show up even when Apple Maps can't be reached: subway and light rail stations, train stations or piers, but not bus stops. When Apple Maps finds no place in the United States for what you typed, the app says so and suggests a station name. When text with commas (a name followed by a city and state) finds nothing, the app asks Apple Maps again without the last part and lists first the results that mention the words it left out.
- The place picker shows your favorites and recent places as cards beside a map. Click the star on a favorite to remove it; a recent place has a star to save it as a favorite and × to remove it. Select a place, then click **Use This Place**, or double-click it. **Open Larger Map** shows a larger map in the same window.
- After you remove a favorite or a recent place, click **Undo** at the bottom of the window or press ⌘Z within 10 seconds to put it back. **Clear** asks before it clears your recent places.
- One place picker window is shared by the main window and Plan a Trip.

## Plan a Trip

Plan a Trip opens in its own window, from the top of the sidebar or the View menu. What you enter stays while the app is open, even after you close the window.

Search a place, or click the star button. It lists your first 6 favorites, in the order you set in the place picker, and your 4 newest recent places; **All Places and Map…** opens the place picker for the rest or a point on the map. When some favorites or recent places aren't listed, a line under them says so. When you type a place, favorites and recent places whose names match come first among the suggestions, and the mode's stations are found by name too, as in the place picker. Then choose when you leave (now, in 15, 30 or 60 minutes, or any day and time), how long after that to look (30 minutes to 4 hours), and how far you'll walk (10 to 45 minutes; this changes only Plan a Trip, not Settings). Every station within that walk is listed, nearest first; for buses, only the nearest stops. Each direction shows its next departures in a row: "Leave by 6:14 PM" when you can make it, how many minutes you have to spare when it's tight (for example "2 min to spare"), or "Too soon". Hover over a departure to see where it goes and the full verdict.

When the window is wide enough, a map beside the list shows the place, your walking range and the stations with their walking times. Click a station on the map or in the list to mark it on both. **Open Larger Map** shows a large map in the same window, also before a place is chosen: click it to drop a pin, then **Use This Place** to plan from there; **Back** keeps the place you had.

When you leave within the next 30 minutes, it uses real-time data where available for the whole time range; when you leave later, it uses timetables only and says so. While the window can be seen and the app is started, real-time departures are fetched again at your refresh interval, but not more often than once a minute, and the results show when they were updated; on standby they aren't. Light rail and trolley lines whose real-time data needs an account always use their timetable.

## Maps

A map for each mode with official line colors and every station. It opens in its own window, from the sidebar or the View menu, and follows the mode you choose; you can switch modes at the top of the window too. Show trains on a station brings the main window forward with that station selected, its card in the sidebar lighting up for a moment, and starts the app first if it is on standby. When the place you walk from changes, an open map moves there; if you have moved or zoomed the map yourself, it stays where you put it until you click Recenter, Whole Map or Service Area.

Stations within walking range are circled: a solid ring in the primary range, a dashed ring in the extended range. Beside each one are its walking time and name, the same as in the station list (on the bus map, the five nearest stops). When a label has no room, it shows the minutes only, or waits until you zoom in; hover over it, or select its station, to see the name. Hovering over a station's dot shows its name too, here and on Plan a Trip's map. Drag the compass to turn the map, and click it to face north again. Click a label to see the station. **Nearby stations** (**Nearby bus stops** on the bus map, **Nearby piers** on the ferry map), above the buttons in the top corner, lists the stations in walking range: choose one to select it, as a click on its dot does, also with the keyboard or VoiceOver. Zoom in close and the dots of stations within walking range stop growing, so stations next to each other come apart. Two circles around where you walk from show your walking range: solid for the primary range, dashed for the extended range. They are an estimate from your walk times and walking speed; the station list uses real walking routes. Hide Range hides the circles and the labels, and the app remembers your choice. The service area is outlined with a dashed line, one ring for each metro area. Each area's name is written in the middle of its ring, on two lines when one doesn't fit, and is left out when the map is zoomed out too far for it. Whole Map shows the metro area of the place you walk from.

Your favorites show on every mode's map as gold stars; a favorite that is one of the map's stations is a small star at the corner of its dot. Stars close together on the screen become one with a number: click it to zoom in until they come apart, or to list them when they are at the same spot. Hover over a star to see its name, and click it to see its card: **Use This Place** walks from there, and its star removes the favorite (click **Undo** or press ⌘Z within 10 seconds to put it back). A station's card has a star too, which adds the station to your favorites, and so does the card of the place you walk from (click its pin). Click an empty spot on the map to drop a pin: its card shows the address, with a star and **Use This Place**; clicking elsewhere on the map closes it. In the demo, pins go only in Manhattan. When a star covers a metro area's name, the name moves up or down a little, or is left out for now. The legend lists favorite places while the map shows any.

## Menu bar

- Start / Standby, and see the next departures in both directions.
- Change station with ‹ ›, or click the station's name to pick one of the nearby stations. Click the line with the transit mode to switch to another mode.
- Your commute presets, if any, are a row of buttons under the line with the transit mode.
- At the bottom, a row of six buttons, each with its icon above its name: **Departures** (opens the main window), **Plan a Trip**, **Map**, **Refresh**, **Settings** and **More**. In languages where the names don't fit, only the icons show; pointing at one shows its name. **More**, or a right-click on the menu bar icon, opens the same menu: Start or Standby, Refresh, Open Catch My Train, Settings and Quit.
- The line under the departures says whose live times they are: the operator's short name when there is one ("MTA live"), "Ferry live" for several ferry operators, and "Live" for several others.
- While leave-by reminders are set, a bell with their number is at the left of the top line.

## Widget

- Small, medium and large sizes.
- The same station, range and data as the app.
- Click the widget to open the main window at the nearby stations, even when the window is closed.
- When more than one station is nearby, click the station's name (with › after it) to show the next one. Standby is in the top corner and Refresh at the end of the bottom line, which says whose live times they are, as in the menu bar panel. The large size also lists a few nearby stations; click one to show it.

## Settings

Settings has seven pages, listed at the side of the window: **General**, **Running**, **Privacy**, **Search range**, **Data source**, **Reminders** and **About**. It opens on the page you last looked at.

- **General:** language (see [Languages](#languages)), feet or meters, 12- or 24-hour time, text size, and appearance (System, Light or Dark; System follows the Mac, including its automatic dark mode).
  - Commute presets: up to 4, each with a name (up to 24 characters), where it walks from (your current location or a favorite; the **Walk from** list has a search field) and its mode. The arrows change their order, **Change** edits one, and **Add Preset…** makes a new one. When you remove the favorite a preset walks from, the preset keeps only its name and asks, in orange, for another starting place; putting the favorite back with **Undo** gives the preset its place back. Demo mode has presets of its own: each time it's turned on it starts with three samples (Work, Home and Weekend), and changes to them aren't kept.
  - Keyboard shortcuts, on by default (they can be turned off): each one can be changed to ⌘ and another key, or removed. Nearby and Start / Standby have none until you give them one, because ⌘N and ⌘S are New and Save in most Mac apps.
- **Running:** refresh interval, auto-standby time, how old real-time data can be before a departure shows *Not sure* (180 seconds by default, and never less than the refresh interval plus 1 minute), and Open at login. **Widget** below says whether the widget can read the app's data and when it last ran, and tells you when the widget is still the older version after an update.
- **Privacy:** **Location** has where you walk from and which map **Directions** opens: Apple Maps or Google Maps (Google Maps opens in your browser, and your starting point and the station are sent to Google). **Privacy and permissions** turns off location or network use for Catch My Train only, clears recent places, or shows the first-launch setup again.
- **Search range:** how far ahead to show arrivals, the primary and extended range and the search radius; **Catch judgment** below has the walking speed and the safety buffer.
- **Data source:** at the top, a **Status** section appears only when something is wrong: too many requests, a data source that can't be reached, this Mac's clock being off, or an operator whose new data wasn't used. For that operator it says why, how many stops its data in use and its new data have, and the lines the new data leaves out, with **Report an Issue…**, which opens a new issue on GitHub with the app's and macOS's versions, the operator and the problem filled in; data this version of the app doesn't fully know yet (new stations or lines) gets **Check for Updates…** too. Below it: when each mode's timetables are up to date as of and when they are next checked, any operator with a problem, the day of the latest update and how many changes it brought (**Show Changes…** lists the new, gone and renamed stops and lines, those near your favorites and commute presets first; departure times aren't compared), and the **Source list**, which says where the app downloads each operator's data and can change without an app update. Its metro area menu lists up to five areas in use (your starting point's, Plan a Trip's, your favorites' and those used in the last 30 days), the one you walk from first, then the others, the most recently used first; the one shown is always among them, in place of the fifth when needed. A long name ends in "…" and shows in full when you point at it. **All Areas…** at the end of the menu opens every area by name with the states it is in, and a search field that finds an area by its name, one of its parts (such as Long Island) or a state, in the app's language or in English.
  - Keys and accounts, at the top of the Data source section: the ones some operators need (each metro area's page in Data sources lists them), stored in the Keychain. Only the ones the chosen metro area needs are listed, each with what it is for there and the other areas that use it too. Each is one key or account, saved once wherever it is used, and the operators' rows below point to it with "(above)". In the NJ Transit account window, a line under the username asks you to type it exactly as NJ Transit's developer site shows it at the top right, including capital letters; it turns orange with a warning sign when what you typed has lowercase letters, but it doesn't change what you typed or stop you from saving. The eye button beside a password or key field shows what you are typing, and a second click hides it again; saved passwords and keys are never shown, and the fields are hidden each time the window opens. Removing one asks first; once you confirm, the app stops using it right away. If the Keychain still holds it, the row says so ("Couldn't remove the account from the Keychain.") and **Remove** stays there to try again; saving a new one also fixes it. Each one is tested when you add it while the app is started; on standby it is saved as not checked yet. While the app is started, a key or account that couldn't be checked is checked again when Keys and accounts shows (at most every 10 minutes); the NJ Transit account is checked by fetching its real-time data once, for rail and for buses separately, without signing in again while the last sign-in still works. Because NJ Transit allows only a few sign-ins a day, after it refuses the account the app waits longer before each new try (30 minutes, then 1, 2, 4 and 8 hours, then once a day), and when the day's sign-ins are used up, it waits until midnight New York time. If one is refused, Settings says so (with the reply from Swiftly, NJ Transit's reply when it says more than that it refused, and an explanation for NJ Transit accounts limited to its test system).
  - Downloaded data: each metro area with downloaded data, why it is kept (your starting point, a Plan a Trip place or your favorites are in it) or the day it is removed if you don't use it again, how much space it takes, and **Remove…** (grayed out for the area of your starting point or a Plan a Trip place). With more than five areas, five have a row (your starting point's, Plan a Trip's, then those taking the most space) and the rest share one, **Other areas: N**, with the space they take and **Show All…**, which opens them all with a search field; areas you removed are listed only there. Your favorites and recent places in a removed area stay, and its data is downloaded again when you choose a place there. Companies that run in more than one area, such as Amtrak, have a row of their own and are kept while one of their areas is. **Delete Downloaded Data…** at the bottom deletes all of it and restarts the app (on standby, with network use allowed; when it can't, the reason shows under it).
  - Demo: see [Demo mode](#demo-mode).
- **Reminders:** how many minutes before the latest time to leave a reminder comes (1 to 30, 2 by default), whether macOS allows notifications, and the reminders set, with **Cancel All Reminders…**, which asks first.
- **About:** the version, update checks (**Check Now**, and whether to check automatically), and **How It Works**.
- A note elsewhere in the app that says to change something in Settings (a key to add, network or location use turned off, Demo mode, the walking range, an operator's new data not used) has a **Settings…** button that opens Settings at that place.
- **Running**, **Search range** and **Catch judgment** each have a button that resets them to the defaults, and the keyboard shortcuts have **Restore Defaults**.

## Leave-by reminders

- Click a departure you can try for: its details say when you have to leave at the latest (**Leave by …**), and **Remind Me at …** sets a notification that many minutes before (Settings → Reminders). Once it's set, the button says **Cancel Reminder**, and the departure's row says "Reminder at …" in blue. When a reminder can't be set, the button is grayed out and a line under it says why: the departure isn't *Can try*, its latest time to leave is too close, 10 reminders are already set, or Demo mode is on.
- The notification names the train, its time, the station and where it goes, and when its time was last checked. It moves when the train's time changes. Other notifications say when the train is now tight, too soon to catch, cancelled or no longer listed.
- The bell in the main window's toolbar and the menu bar panel opens the list of reminders, soonest first: click one to see its station, click × to cancel it, or **Cancel All Reminders…**.
- While a reminder is still to come, automatic standby waits until its latest time to leave, and the Standby button says so ("On until … for a reminder"). Standby you choose yourself still happens at once.
- macOS asks whether Catch My Train may send notifications the first time you set one. A Focus can hide the banner; to always see it, allow Catch My Train in that Focus in System Settings.

## Languages

English plus 22 more languages, including fully mirrored layouts for languages written right to left. **Language** in Settings → General opens a list with a search field. It starts with **System Language**, which follows the Mac's preferred languages (the first one the app has, otherwise English) and is checked again each time the app opens; a new install uses it. **Suggested** then lists up to three of your Mac's languages that the app has, and **All languages** the rest. Each language is written in its own name, followed in gray by its name in the language the app is in, and the list is in the alphabetical order of those names (in English, the order of the table in the README). The search finds a language by its own name, its name in the app's language, its English name or its code. The install guide in the DMG follows the system language too. Each regional variant uses its language's one translation: Spanish from Spain or Mexico gets Latin American Spanish, Portuguese from Portugal gets Brazilian Portuguese, and British English gets English. Updates keep the language you already use.

## Demo mode

Turn on **Demo mode** in Settings → Data source (the Demo section at the bottom) to see made-up subway, bus, train and ferry times in Manhattan, marked "Demo data only". It starts at Times Square, and you can choose another place in Manhattan: the place picker's search finds the demo's places, and you can drop a pin anywhere in Manhattan. It doesn't use your location or download any timetables, and walk times are straight-line estimates. On standby, demo departures don't turn *Not sure*, because made-up times never go out of date; only the map comes from Apple Maps. Places, favorites and recent places you choose in the demo are kept only until you turn it off; your own places are kept. Settings you change during the demo stay changed. The transit mode goes back to the one you used before the demo. Plan a Trip works in the demo too: its search finds the demo's places and Manhattan's subway stations, and **All Places and Map…** under the star button opens the demo's place picker. Its times are made up like the main window's, so a station shows the same times in both; as in the real app, they are shown as real-time when you leave within the next 30 minutes and as timetable times when you leave later.
